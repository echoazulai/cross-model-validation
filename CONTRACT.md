## 1. Purpose

Claude and Codex provide independent authoring and validation. The author and judge are always
from different model families. A judge receives the work, task context, acceptance criteria, and
bridge-recorded evidence, but not the author's conclusions.

## 2. Authority

- Either Claude or Codex may author work.
- Standard and Full deliverables are judged by the other model family.
- For Standard and Full work, PASS precedes presentation to the owner; a pending receipt
  is not permission to disclose the provisional answer.
- The author never merges its own reviewed work.
- Models do not silently overrule one another.
- Unresolved disagreement goes to the owner.
- Full-tier work always requires owner acceptance.

## 3. Work tiers

- Light: mechanical documentation, formatting, or configuration work.
- Standard: routine code, research, or shippable standalone artefacts.
- Full: consequential, security-sensitive, health-related, or architectural work.

Standard and Full work require cross-model validation before merge or presentation as complete.

Validation is also a presentation gate. Standard and Full findings, recommendations,
plans, code, reports, conclusions, and other deliverables must not be presented to the
owner before the exact artefact or underlying deliverable has a final PASS verdict.
Pending, NEEDS-CHANGES, and FAIL states permit status or blocker reporting only, not
disclosure of the provisional deliverable. Light conversation and non-substantive
status messages remain exempt. The owner may explicitly waive this gate for a named
task under the existing waiver rule.
This presentation gate does not replace or weaken the separate requirement for the
owner's explicit acceptance of Full-tier reviewed work.

## 4. Authoring repository work

1. Work on `claude/<slug>` or `codex/<slug>`.
2. Run the relevant deterministic tests.
3. Ensure tracked working-tree changes are committed.
4. Record the base branch, base SHA, author branch, and reviewed SHA.
5. Record untracked files in the manifest.
6. Submit acceptance criteria describing requirements rather than conclusions.
7. Do not change the submitted branch after dropping it for review.

Tracked modifications at submission block the drop. Untracked files are recorded but do not
automatically block it.

## 5. Authoring standalone artefacts

1. Place required files in a bounded directory.
2. Exclude caches, generated bytecode, credentials, and unrelated material.
3. Copy the payload into an immutable review package.
4. Record a manifest of paths, sizes, and SHA-256 hashes.
5. Record a package-level payload hash.

Copying or hashing failure aborts submission. Such errors must never be suppressed.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

Acceptance criteria state observable requirements:

- Required behaviour and important edge cases.
- Expected failure behaviour.
- Compatibility, privacy, and security constraints.
- Declared deterministic checks.
- What those checks claim to prove.
- The requirement that tests genuinely prove their claims and are not tautological.

The judge determines whether the criteria are met.

## 7. Immutable repository review

The bridge creates a detached temporary worktree at the recorded reviewed SHA. The judge reviews:

- The exact `base_sha..reviewed_sha` diff.
- Files at the reviewed SHA.
- Bridge-recorded deterministic check evidence for that SHA.

The author's live working tree is never the review target.

If the branch head or reviewed identity changes after submission, the verdict is stale and cannot
merge.

## 8. Deterministic checks

The judge remains read-only.

Checks declared in the package manifest are executed by bridge plumbing before judging. The bridge
records:

- Command exactly as executed.
- Start and finish timestamps.
- Exit code and timeout status.
- Trimmed stdout and stderr.
- Path or hash for any retained full log.

These records are evidence supplied to the judge. A model's statement that it ran a command is a
claim, not execution proof.

The bridge does not automatically execute arbitrary commands proposed by a judge.

## 9. Independent judging

The judge must:

1. Inspect the actual package or detached worktree.
2. Inspect the exact submitted diff for repository work.
3. Evaluate bridge-recorded check evidence.
4. Look for missing edge cases and contract violations.
5. Decide whether tests prove their claims.
6. Report unsupported claims and untested behaviour.
7. Return a structured verdict only.

## 10. Verdict contract

Both lanes enforce the same JSON schema.

Required fields:

- `verdict`: `pass`, `needs-changes`, or `fail`.
- `summary`.
- `findings`, each with severity, location, and evidence-backed detail.
- `checked`, treated as a structured claim about inspected material.
- `executions`, empty when the judge ran no commands.
- `reviewed_identity`, with non-applicable repository or artefact identity fields set to `null`.

A judge's `executions` array is a structured claim, not evidence. Bridge-recorded
check results are authoritative execution evidence.

Malformed verdicts are bridge failures and can never become PASS.

## 11. Escalation

If Claude is unavailable because of quota, authentication, session limits, or another
availability failure, Codex reports only that blocker and asks the owner to choose:
(a) wait for Claude; (b) authorise the standard-tier judge to validate full-tier-authored work; or (c) name
another independent model for the task. Codex never selects the fallback automatically. The
alternate review must remain read-only, bind its verdict to the exact reviewed identity,
retain review evidence, and be named in the visible review receipt. No substantive work
is presented until the owner-authorised validator returns PASS.

- A lower-tier PASS stands at its declared tier.
- A lower-tier `needs-changes` or `fail` is reviewed once by the Full-tier judge.
- Initial and escalated verdicts are retained.
- Cross-tier disagreement is recorded.
- The author addresses accepted findings in a new commit and submits a new package.
- Previous verdicts remain in the audit trail.

## 12. Merge-on-pass

Automatic merge is allowed only when:

- The final verdict is PASS.
- The reviewed identity matches the submitted identity.
- Required bridge-run checks passed.
- The author branch still points to the reviewed SHA.
- The destination update is fast-forward only.
- The package has not already merged.
- No unresolved high-severity finding remains.

The bridge pushes the exact reviewed SHA. No descendant or live working-tree state may substitute
for it.

Standard-tier merge-on-pass remains default-off until payload hashing, bridge-run checks, strict
identity validation, and their independent tests are complete. It may then be enabled per package.

Full-tier work never auto-completes without owner acceptance.

## 13. Package lifecycle and recovery

Packages move through:

```text
created -> ready -> claimed -> reviewed -> recorded -> archived
```

Each claim records its worker, timestamp, and lease expiry.

- Expired claims are reclaimable.
- Existing valid output is preserved.
- Processing is idempotent.
- Duplicate reviews may be retained, but notices and merges occur once.
- Authentication, quota, or missing/invalid judge-output failures move packages to retryable
  `held`, not `failed`; malformed trust-boundary inputs are held as non-retryable.
- Temporary worktrees are pruned during polling and lease recovery.

## 14. Audit trail

The bridge retains:

- Original manifest and acceptance criteria.
- Reviewed SHA or payload hash.
- Declared checks and bridge-recorded results.
- Initial, escalated, and final verdicts.
- Merge result.
- Owner disposition where recorded.
- Who-catches-what classification.
- Final archive location.

Notices point to archive locations, not former queue paths.

## 15. Governance wording

Shared governance should state:

> Claude and Codex may both author work. Standard and Full deliverables are judged by the other
> model family. Expansion of either model's responsibility remains evidence-led and is reviewed
> through the who-catches-what log.

## 16. Current implementation boundary

Implemented as of 2026-07-10:

- Detached review worktrees in both lanes.
- Base-SHA and reviewed-SHA capture.
- Dirty tracked-tree refusal.
- Untracked-file inventory recording.
- Fail-closed artefact copying, deterministic payload inventories and SHA-256 binding.
- Strict request, verdict and notification validation in both lanes.
- Manifest-declared checks in disposable no-network Bubblewrap roots.
- Bounded bridge-owned check results and retained, hashed logs.
- Verdict-to-manifest identity binding.
- Forty-five-minute locked, heartbeated claim leases and reclaim.
- Per-package finalisation locks for exactly-once notices, ledger completion and merge authority.
- Direction-aware held metadata, retry policy and collision-safe requeueing.
- Worktree pruning.
- Tiered judging and escalation.
- Full-tier owner gating and fast-forward SHA-pinned merge gates.
- Concurrent, queue-isolated lanes under a non-overlapping two-minute poll lock.
- Reviewed-commit deployment with backup and `DEPLOYED_COMMIT` provenance.
- Deterministic trust-boundary tests, verified against real Bubblewrap during completion audit.

The section 16 completion gate is satisfied. Standard merge-on-pass is technically capable but
remains opt-in per package. Full-tier work still requires owner acceptance.
